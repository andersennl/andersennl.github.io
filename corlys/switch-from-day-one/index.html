Switching from Day One to Corlys

Corlys is a journal for iPhone and iPad. If you keep a journal in Day One and want your entries to be more than an archive, this page tells you what moves over, what does not, and why you might switch.

Three reasons to switch

Your entries can leave the app. Every new entry can go to a webhook you choose: into an Obsidian daily note, a task manager, an n8n workflow. Typed or dictated, it arrives as plain text or Markdown, in your own words. How webhooks work

Speech stays on the device. Dictation is transcribed on your iPhone by Apple’s speech recognition. There is no account. Entries sync through your private iCloud, and the only thing that leaves is what you set up yourself.

The journal is free. Writing, dictation, transcripts, calendar, map, photos, import and webhooks cost nothing.

What the import brings over

Export your journal in Day One as JSON (Settings → Import/Export). In Corlys, open Settings → Import from Day One and pick the ZIP file or the unpacked folder.

From Day OneIn Corlys
Date and timeKept
Text with formattingKept as headings, bold, italic, lists
TitleTaken from the first heading
LocationKept, shown on the map
PhotosKept, resized to 2048 pixels on the long side
Audio recordingsKept, playable in the entry
Videos, PDFsNot imported
TagsNot imported
WeatherNot imported
Several journalsImported into one journal

You can run the import again later. Entries that are already there are skipped. Imported entries are not sent to your webhooks.

What you will miss

Corlys runs on Apple devices only. There is no Android app, no web app, no video in entries, no tags and no printed books. If you rely on one of these, Day One is the better choice for you.

What you gain

  • A widget on the home or lock screen that starts dictation directly.
  • A transcript that stays word for word what you said. The app does not summarise or rewrite.
  • A short title for every entry, written by Apple Intelligence.
  • Timeline, calendar and map, the way you know them.
