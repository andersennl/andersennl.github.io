Paste Again for macOS

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 28 July 2026

Paste Again is a menu bar app for macOS that keeps a searchable history of text you copy. It is developed by Nikolaj Andersen, an individual developer.

The short version: Paste Again stores your copied text on your own Mac and nowhere else. There is no account, no server, no analytics, and no network connection.

Data the app collects

Paste Again collects only what it needs to show you your own clipboard history. While recording is active, the app checks the macOS system clipboard about three times per second and records a new entry whenever the clipboard content changes.

For each entry, the app stores:

  • The copied text. This is the plain-text content of your clipboard. Because you decide what to copy, this text can contain anything you copy — including personal information, passwords, or other sensitive content.
  • A timestamp of when the entry was recorded.
  • The name and bundle identifier of the app that was in the foreground when you copied (for example, “Safari” and com.apple.Safari). This is shown in the history list so you can tell entries apart. If macOS does not report a foreground app, the entry records “Unknown App”.
  • A random identifier generated locally for the entry, used only to tell entries apart inside the app.

What the app does not collect

  • Text only. Paste Again records plain text. Images, files, and other non-text clipboard content are not recorded.
  • Content marked as private is skipped. Password managers and similar apps mark what they put on the clipboard with a “concealed”, “transient”, or “auto-generated” marker, following the convention documented at nspasteboard.org. Paste Again checks for these markers and does not record such content at all. This depends on the other app setting the marker correctly, so it cannot be guaranteed for every app.
  • Empty entries are skipped. Clipboard content that is empty or contains only whitespace is not recorded.
  • No content from other apps. The app does not read documents, windows, screen content, or anything else from other applications. It reads only the system clipboard.
  • No personal identifiers. The app does not collect your name, email address, contacts, location, device identifiers, advertising identifiers, or any account information. There is no sign-up and no login.
  • No usage data. There is no analytics, telemetry, crash-reporting SDK, or tracking of any kind. The app contains no advertising and does no tracking as defined by Apple’s App Tracking Transparency framework.

Alongside your history, the app stores two settings on your Mac: whether recording is enabled, and your chosen keyboard shortcut. These contain no personal data.

How the data is used

The recorded entries are used for one purpose only: to display your clipboard history inside the app so you can search it and paste an earlier entry.

Specifically, the stored data is used to:

  • show the history list and its search results,
  • write a selected entry back to the system clipboard,
  • paste that entry into the app you were previously using.

The data is not used for any other purpose. It is not profiled, analysed, aggregated, sold, or used for advertising, personalisation, or training any model.

Where the data is stored

Everything is stored locally on your Mac, inside the app’s own sandbox container under ~/Library/Containers. The container folder is named after the app’s bundle identifier, and the history sits inside it under Data/Library/Application Support.

The history is a plain JSON file. It is not encrypted by the app itself. It is protected by the standard macOS file permissions of your user account and by the App Sandbox, which prevents other sandboxed apps from reading it. If you have FileVault enabled, the file is covered by your Mac’s full-disk encryption while the disk is locked.

The app runs in the macOS App Sandbox and does not request the network entitlement, so it is not able to make network connections.

Third parties

No third party receives any of your data. No data ever leaves your device.

  • Paste Again contains no networking code and holds no network entitlement. It cannot upload your history.
  • There are no third-party SDKs, libraries, analytics providers, advertising networks, or cloud services in the app. The app is built only against Apple’s own system frameworks.
  • The developer receives nothing. Nikolaj Andersen has no access to your clipboard history and no way to obtain it.
  • Your history is not synced to iCloud or any other service by the app.

The only outbound action in the app is a link in the Settings window to the app’s website. If you click it, macOS opens that address in your default web browser. Nothing about your clipboard is sent, and it only happens when you click.

If you obtained Paste Again from the App Store, Apple may provide the developer with the aggregate sales and crash statistics it collects for all App Store apps, under Apple’s own privacy policy and subject to the sharing choices you made in your macOS privacy settings. This is handled entirely by Apple; it is separate from the app and contains no clipboard content.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Accessibility (post events). This permission is used for exactly one thing: sending a synthetic Cmd+V keystroke to the app you were using, so a selected entry is pasted for you. It is not used to read anything from other apps. If you do not grant it, the app still works — selecting an entry copies it to your clipboard, and you paste it yourself.
  • Login item. Starting at login is off by default. The app only adds itself to your login items while you have “Start Paste Again at login” checked in its Settings window. You can uncheck it there at any time, or remove the entry in System Settings under General → Login Items.

Retention and deletion

  • Item limit. The app keeps at most the 1,000 most recent entries. When a new entry is added beyond that, the oldest entry is dropped automatically. Copying text that is already in the history moves the existing entry to the top rather than creating a duplicate.
  • No time limit. Entries are not deleted after a set period. They stay until they are pushed out by the 1,000-entry limit or until you delete them.
  • Clearing the history. Choose Clear History from the menu bar icon. This removes every entry immediately, from memory and from the stored file.
  • Deleting everything. Uninstalling the app and deleting its container folder in ~/Library/Containers removes the history file and all settings.

Because nothing is stored anywhere except your own Mac, there is no server-side copy, no backup held by the developer, and no separate deletion request to make. Deleting the data on your Mac deletes it completely. If you want data deleted, you can do it yourself in the ways described above; the developer cannot do it for you because the developer never has it.

Withdrawing consent

You are in control at all times:

  • Pause recording. Choose Pause Recording from the menu bar icon. The app stops reading the clipboard until you resume it. This setting persists across restarts.
  • Revoke Accessibility access. Remove Paste Again in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility. Paste-back stops working; the rest of the app continues to work.
  • Stop automatic launch. Uncheck “Start Paste Again at login” in the app’s Settings window, or disable Paste Again in System Settings → General → Login Items.
  • Remove the app. Quit Paste Again and delete it. With its container folder deleted, no data remains.

Pausing or revoking a permission does not delete entries already recorded — use Clear History for that.

Children

Paste Again is a general-purpose utility. It does not knowingly collect data from anyone, including children, beyond the local clipboard history described above, and it transmits nothing.
